Follow three movements.

1. Food drips in
COB is the amount waiting. The drops show how fast glucose arrives.
2. The blue valve opens
IOB is an amount, not an opening. Delayed insulin action moves the valve.
3. The level responds
More in than out: it rises. More out than in: it falls. Balanced flows: it holds.

Watch IOB and the valve.

The insulin reservoir can get smaller while the valve is still opening. Insulin clears over time, not one drop per glucose particle.

The small side pipes keep liver supply and other glucose use separate. Glints at DEX's feet show activity.

Developer notes & model readings

Column height follows true blood glucose, not CGM. The valve is a visual metaphor for delayed x1 transport action relative to its starting baseline, not a literal anatomical valve, percentage of active insulin or combined insulin effect. Its bounded opening does not calculate the glucose flow or BG. Basal action can keep it partly open with zero rapid IOB.

Glucose particles follow separate food appearance, liver output, net blood-to-tissue transport, background use and renal loss. Negative transport reverses the particles. The blue wire carries an action signal, not glucose or a stoichiometric stream of insulin. Tissue disposal and exercise act in Q2 and are not subtracted again from the blood column. Insulin also influences liver output; that effect remains in the liver stream. Particle density is bounded for readability, not a count of molecules.

The food reservoir uses actual model gut contents (D1 + D2), expressed as grams of glucose equivalent. The main game's COB is a separate time-based estimate; it is not changed by this draft. The four recordings are unchanged; no personal data, dosing inputs or recommendations are added.
